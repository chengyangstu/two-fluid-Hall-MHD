  ABSTRACT

   The origin of extreme and anomalous ion heating during magnetic reconnection has been a long-standing mystery. It is shown in this paper
    that, at the core of the process, the stochastic heating mechanism is responsible for this energization. The in-plane Hall electric ﬁelds that
    intrinsically develop during collisionless reconnection not only accelerate ions but also thermalize them by inducing chaotic motion. The
   mechanism explains various observed features of ion heating, e.g., its relation to the guide ﬁeld strength and the ion mass-to-charge ratio.

                                                                            VI. DISCUSSION

                                                                            In Sec. I, ﬁve main features of ion heating during collisionless
                                                                         reconnection were identiﬁed. Each of these features will now be indi-
                                                                                vidually examined in relation to the stochastic heating mechanism.

                                                                                        1. The association of anomalous ion heating with Hall electric ﬁelds.
                                                                                                    It has been shown that Hall electric ﬁelds intrinsically satisfy the
                                                                                  stochastic ion heating criterion during  collisionless magnetic
                                                                         reconnection for up to moderate guide ﬁelds (bg    0:5); thus,
                                                                            the electric ﬁelds, render ion motions chaotic and effectively heat
                                                                            the species. In particular, Hall electric ﬁelds and ion heating were
                                                                  measured in  situ in the Magnetic Reconnection Experiment
                                                               (MRX)10  during  a  driven  collisionless  reconnection  event.
                                                                                Inserting the measured values of E   700 V/m, B   0:01 T, and
                                                                         a length scale of  1 cm yields a ¼ 14:6 > 1, and so the fact that
                                                                           Hall electric ﬁelds induce stochastic ion heating is well evidenced
                                                                  by experiments.
                                                                                        2. Violation of the magnetic moment l conservation law. Stochastic
                                                                            heating is a mechanism wherein the guiding-center approxima-
                                                                               tion breaks down as shown in Sec. II, in which case the magnetic
                                                        moment l is obviously not conserved. In fact, it was shown via
                                                                                        test-particle simulations32 that regions where l is not conserved
                                                                            coincide with regions where the stochastic ion heating condition
                                                                                                      is satisﬁed and that initially close particles diverge in time—i.e.,
                                                                            the system has a positive Lyapunov exponent.
                                                                                        3. Temperature anisotropy. Stochastic heating  is associated with
                                                                           perpendicular motion and thus with perpendicular temperature.
                                                             The perpendicular energy gain, however, could eventually be
                                                                                 redistributed in the parallel direction through other means such
                                                                               as collisions, waves, and/or instabilities.
