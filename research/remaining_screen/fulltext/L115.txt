                               Abstract
  Particle in cell (PIC) simulations of tearing modes in two-dimensional plane geometry in a force
free reversed field pinch (RFP)  like plasma equilibrium are performed to study possible kinetic
effects on these modes in RFPs.  Linear tearing modes are compared in the PIC and two fluid
models. The results showed that the growth rates and the profiles of magnetic field components
in the two models are very similar, indicating that the kinetic effects on the tearing modes are
weak such that the two fluid approximation is rather accurate for modeling these instabilities in
RFPs. During the nonlinear evolution of the tearing mode in this geometry small scale secondary
instabilities located near the internal layer of the primary tearing instability are excited. These
secondary instabilities appear to be driven by the nonlinearly induced local pressure gradient in
the regions of unfavorable curvature of the nonlinearly evolved magnetic field. They could also
appear in a realistic RFP geometry and play a role during sawtooth crashes in these machines.

I.  INTRODUCTION

   Tearing instabilities play an important role in laboratory, space and astrophysical plas­
mas, see e. g. Ref.  [1]. They are thought to be responsible for fast reconnection of magnetic
fields, relaxation to the Taylor state and the dynamo effect in reversed field pinch (RFP) [2]­
[6]. It is also believed that the tearing instabilities contribute to the ion heating [7] and the
momentum transport  [8] in RFPs, observed during internal reconnect ion events (sawtooth
crashes) [9]-[16] in these machines. The understanding of the physics of tearing instabilities
is important for the understanding of various processes in RFP.
  The most common models used to study tearing instabilities are single fluid resistive
magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)  [17],[18] and two fluid MHD  [19]-[23].  Recent two fluid
modeling of tearing modes in RFP plasma [21],[24],[25] showed that the two fluid effects
significantly change the modes parameters in comparison with the parameters calculated
with the resistive MHD model. The two fluid effects modify both the modes growth rate
and its polarization.  Analysis of the size of internal layer in a tearing mode relevant to
RFP plasma [7] showed that the spatial scale of velocity, electric field and current profiles
near a resonance surface is comparable to the ion gyroradius. The latter is relatively large
in RFPs because of the smaller equilibrium magnetic field.  Thus, in addition to the two
fluid effects, the effects due to the variation of field components of the tearing mode within
the ion gyroradius, finite Larmor radius (FLR) effects, or other kinetic effects, can influence
these modes in RFP as well.
   In this study we analyze possible influence of the kinetic effects on linear tearing modes in
RFP-like plasma by comparing these modes calculated in a fully kinetic collisionless particle­
in-cell (PIC) model and in the two fluid modeL First principles time evolving particle-in-cell
code VPIC [26] is used in the kinetic modeling. Because of practical limitations of the PIC
simulations we consider the tearing modes in plane geometry in a force free RFP-like plasma
equilibrium bounded by two conducting walls. In this model plasma perturbations depend
on x and y coordinates while z coordinate is ignorable, such that a two-dimensional (in
configuration space) version of VPIC is used. Our results showed that the growth rates and
profiles of magnetic field components of the tearing modes in the two fluid and in the kinetic
models are very similar except for some additional broadening of the PIC profiles near the
resonance surface, indicating that the kinetic effects on the tearing modes in RFP are weak.

While the amplitudes of isotropic and anisotropic parts of the pressure tensor calculated from
PIC results have similar magnitudes, the contribution to the fluid force from the isotropic
part of the pressure tensor significantly exceeds the one from the anisotropic part making
the fluid approximation to be rather accurate for modeling of the tearing modes in RFP.
   In addition to the analysis of the linear tearing modes in the fully kinetic model, we use
VPIC to study nonlinear time evolution of the tearing modes in this geometry. The use of
first principles PIC code is a powerful tool in the search for new effects which can be relevant
to RFP discharges and which could be undetected by the present experimental diagnostics.
In contrast to a cylindrical model, which requires 3-D simulations, there is no self-maintained
(by dynamo) RFP-like equilibrium in the plane geometry.  In our simulations an initially
imposed unstable RFP-like equilibrium is transformed during the nonlinear evolution of
the tearing mode into a new equilibrium which  is topologically different from the initial
equilibrium. Due to this reason we concentrate on the initial stage of the nonlinear mode
evolution to find possible new effects that can be relevant to the tearing modes in RFP. In our
simulations we observed the excitation of small scale secondary instabilities located near the
internal layer of the primary tearing instability. We identify these secondary instabilities
as the ones driven by the nonlinearly induced local pressure gradient in the regions of
unfavorable curvature of the nonlinearly evolved magnetic field. Such secondary instabilities
can also appear in a realistic RFP geometry and playa role during sawtooth crashes in these
machines. More detailed studies of these instabilities are the subject of the future work.
  The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II plasma geometry, its equilibrium and simu­
lation parameters are discussed. In Sec. III the results of PIC simulations are presented and
analyzed. We summarize in Sec. IV.

III.  RESULTS OF PARTICLE IN CELL SIMULATIONS

  PIC simulations are carried out on a multi processor supercomputer using approximately
109 particles. The tearing eigenmode growth from the computational noise present in the
PIC model. The time evolution of volume integrated contributions to magnetic energy and
kinetic energy of electrons and ions is presented in Fig. 3. Each quantity is normalized on the
initial volume integrated magnetic energy. The total energy (electromagnetic plus kinetic)
in the system is conserved with a very good accuracy. The initial kinetic energy of electrons
contains the contribution due to their drift motion in the equilibrium current. Some initial
oscillations in these time dependencies are because the system is not in an exact kinetic
eqUilibrium at t =  O.  Equilibrium magnetic field has only By and Bz components such
that the initial magnetic energy in the Ex component is zero in this figure. The dominant
tearing mode corresponds to the m = 1 harmonics.  This mode grows  first linearly and
then nonlinearly until the system saturates in a new quasi equilibrium. The transition from
the initial to the final state is accompanied by a strong modification of the magnetic field
geometry along with significant compression and rarefication of plasma in different parts of
the domain. In the final state plasma is almost stationary (no ion flows) and the increase of
kinetic energies of ions and electrons during the transition is due to compressional plasma
heating in the regions with the increased pressure.

which is close to the growth rate of this mode calculated in the two fluid model, ITA = 0.7 for
kya = 2 in Fig. 2. Comparison of magnetic field components in the linear tearing eigenmode
in the two models is made in Fig. 4. The data for the PIC results are taken at t/TA = 2.8

MHD model by imposing a small pressure peak near a resonance surface on top of the
uniform equilibrium pressure profile with the peak's parameters similar to the local maxima

unfavorable curvature. In this analysis a spectrum of unstable modes with the growth rates
and perpendicular wave numbers in the range similar to the observed in these secondary
instabilities is found. This simplified modeling supports our conclusion about the source of
the observed secondary instabilities in the PIC simulations. These instabilities can appear
in realistic RFP geometry when the local pressure gradient is created by the tearing mode.
The unfavorable curvature is already embedded in the magnetic equilibrium in this case.
Because these instabilities are accompanied by small scale plasma velocity perturbations,
see Fig. 6(e), they can contribute to the ion heating observed at sawtooth crashes in RFPs.
More detailed studies of possible effects of these instabilities on RFP plasma is subject of
the future work.

IV. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS

   Simulations of tearing modes in two-dimensional plane geometry in RFP-like plasma
equilibrium are performed in the PIC modeL  Parameters of linear tearing eigenmodes in
the PIC and two fluid plasma models in these geometry are compared. The results showed
that the growth rates and profiles of magnetic field components in the two models are very
similar except for some broadening of the PIC tearing mode near the resonance surface
when compared with the two fluid mode.  Thus the kinetic effects on the tearing modes
are relatively weak and the two fluid approximation is rather accurate for modeling these
instabilities in RFPs.
   Nonlinear time evolution of the tearing mode in the PIC model is also studied in this
geometry. The conclusion, made in previous studies based on resistive MHD model, that
the plasma flow velocity in nonlinearly evolved tearing mode can be a sizable portion of the
ion thermal velocity is confirmed in our results. Small scale secondary instabilities, located
near the internal layer of the primary tearing instability, are found in our simulations.
These secondary instabilities are driven by the nonlinearly induced local pressure gradient
in the regions of unfavorable curvature of the nonlinearly evolved magnetic field. They can
also appear in a realistic RFP geometry and playa role during sawtooth crashes in these
machines. More detailed studies of these instabilities are subject of the future work.
