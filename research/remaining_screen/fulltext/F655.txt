                          Electron-only reconnection (EREC) is a magnetic reconnection regime occurring within subion-
                          scale current sheets (CSs), exhibiting only electron jets, without any ion outflows. EREC has been
                               first observed in the Earth’s magnetosheath, where its occurrence is linked to the small correlation2026
                       length of magnetic fluctuations, limiting the growth of CSs to very large scales. On the other
                      hand, the development of EREC in open systems with large magnetic correlation lengths, such as
                       the solar wind (SW), remains an open question. To address this problem, we employ a large-scale
                 2D hybrid simulation with finite electron inertia, investigating the development of EREC drivenMay
                    by turbulence. By injecting energy at very large scales, we allow EREC to develop spontaneously

                        turbulence-driven mechanisms:  (1) secondary EREC induced by the interaction of plasmoids in
                       the outflows of large-scale ion-coupled reconnection; (2) EREC directly driven at subion scales by
                       the electron Kelvin-Helmholtz instability in small-scale velocity shears. Furthermore, we perform
                     a statistical analysis of CSs using the machine-learning clustering algorithm HDBSCAN, showing
                       that subion-scale CSs capable of hosting EREC are dominant in our simulation. Our results suggest
                       that EREC could occur even in large-scale space and astrophysical systems, like the SW, driven by
                      secondary turbulent processes, potentially playing a key role in dissipating energy at kinetic scales.

                 Appendix A: Calculation of inflow Alfv´en velocity and inflow density

  In this appendix, we describe the calculation of the inflow Alfv´en velocity, cAi,in = Bin/√4πminin, used to normalize
the reconnection outflow velocities in Figures 2, 3, and 6.  Here, Bin and nin denote the magnitude of the inflow
magnetic field and the inflow density, respectively. To quantify these parameters, we analyze a 1D cut along the
inflow direction, defined as the eigenvector associated with the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian of the magnetic flux
function at the X-point. The IREC and EREC cut lengths are set to ∼12di and ∼2di, respectively, to appropriately
match the width of the reconnection exhaust in both cases. Bin is calculated as the average of the local magnetic field
maxima found on both sides of the X-point (s=0), as illustrated in Figure 9. The inflow density nin is defined as the
mean density along this 1D cut. As shown in Figure 10, nin remains close to the initial background density, n0.
