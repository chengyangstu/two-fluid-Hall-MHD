                  Reconnection in 2D Inertial Magnetohydrodynamics

                        Lydia Boubendir, Peter Constantin, Hezekiah Grayer II, and Zhongtian Hu2026
                         ABSTRACT. We consider two-fluid equations of inertial magnetohydrodynamics in two
                                   spatial dimensions. These equations can be recast as the evolution of two active scalars,
                             with fluid-like interaction kernels. The equations come in two flavors, left-handed andOct
                                right-handed. They are conservative and have global unique solutions for both smooth
                                       initial data and for initial data in Yudovich class L1(R2) ∩L∞(R2). We prove that the16                                   inviscid limit holds in Yudovich class. We prove the persistence of C2,α regularity of patch
                               boundaries through merger events for the left-handed equations. We finally show numerical
                            examples of magnetic reconnection, validating known rigorous results and uncovering new
                                  cases.
[math.AP]                                                       1. Introduction

                    Magnetic reconnection, a dynamical change of topology of the magnetic field lines, is a
                  physical phenomenon of great importance to magnetohydrodynamics and plasma physics
                     [3], [13]. The equations of ideal magnetohydrodynamics do not permit topology change
                   in the absence of singularities. Magnetic resistivity can be responsible for a change of
                  topology, but its time scale is in general too slow to explain the observed phenomena. A
               more rapid mechanism which does not require either singularities or resistivity was proposed
                    in the physics literature [4], [6], [12]. The mechanism is due to inertia of electrons and it is
                  described using fluid-like models with two fluids. The models are Hamiltonian, are derived
                from action principles and have been studied numerically, but not by rigorous mathematics,
                  as far as we know, before the work [9]. That work was devoted to two-dimensional theory
                and proved regularity results and topological change due to merger of two active scalars.
                  The present paper is a continuation of [9]. We consider two-dimensional inertial magne-arXiv:2610.23494v1             tohydrodynamics where evolution of the electronic vorticity ω is coupled to the evolution of
                   the magnetic canonical momentum F. The system of equations is

                        (∂tF + {ψ, F} + {ϕ, ω} = 0,
                                                                                                              (1)
                                         ∂tω + {ψ, ω} + {ϕ, F} = 0,

in R2, where {f, g} = ∂1f∂2g −∂2f∂1g is the Poisson bracket. The electron fluid’s stream
function ψ is related to the vorticity by

                      ∆ψ = ω,                                       (2)

and the magnetic potential ϕ is related to the magnetic canonical momentum by

                    −∆ϕ + ϕ = ±F.                                   (3)

The different ± cases are distinct, but are both conservative and derived from action principles.
In [9] and in the present work, the (+) case is called right-handed, and the (−) case is called
left-handed. Although only the right-handed system was considered in the physics literature
[6], [12], both cases are mathematically interesting, yet quite different. Both systems can be
re-written by a linear change of the dependent variables as evolution systems of two active
scalars. Active scalars are real valued functions of space and time which evolve in time
by pure transport, carried by incompressible velocities they instantaneously create. The
velocities are computed from the scalars using time-independent, constant coefficient, and
(in the present case) linear equations of state. The Yudovich class is the class of active scalars
whereby (both) the scalars are bounded and integrable in space. Patches are particular cases,
where the scalars are indicator functions of bounded domains in R2. The Yudovich class is
preserved in time, and weak solutions exist and are unique in it [9]. Each of the two flavors
(right-handed and left-handed) has screened and unscreened versions. In the unscreened
versions (3) is replaced by −∆ϕ = ±F, i.e. the term ϕ is dropped. (That term physically
corresponds to screening by an electronic skin depth).
   The key feature of interest in the model (1) is the prediction of magnetic reconnection,
i.e., topological change of the magnetic field B = ∇⊥ϕ, without resistivity. Until recently,
this feature was only captured in numerical simulations [6]. In [9] the global well-posedness
of (1) was obtained both in the Yudovich class and in the smooth category. Topological
change was shown to occur in both the case of smooth solutions and patch solutions, and the
results were for both the right-handed and left-handed systems.
    In this paper we prove the convergence of solutions of vanishing resistivity to the
solutions of the inertial MHD system (1). This is done by adapting the method of [8] to the
case of two fluids. The result of [8] was for the two dimensional torus T2, and extending it
to the whole space presents certain challenges. One of the key elements of the result of [8]
was the exponential integrability of the gradient of velocity. In Yudovich class the velocity is
not Lipschitz-continuous, and the exponential integrability is a weaker surrogate for that. In
the whole space, obviously there cannot be a global exponential integrability, and we have
to localize the result. In addition, the decay of velocities is too slow for energy methods,
and the velocities do not have self-contained equations like in the Euler and Navier-Stokes
equations. We use methods form [10] to control decay at spatial infinity of the active scalars,
in the resistive case, and overcome thus the technical obstructions to global Yudovich class
zero resistivity limit. The proof is presented in Section 3.
   The left-handed unscreened system consists of a pair of incompressible Euler equations
which do not interact with each other. As it is known [5], [7], in the Euler equations vortex
patch boundaries stay smooth through the evolution, if they start smooth. In Section 4 we
show, for the left-handed screened system, that C1,α- and C2,α-regularity of patch boundaries
persists globally, even through merger.

   We conduct a numerical study of reconnection for patch solutions. In Section 5 we
describe the algorithm, and in Section 6 we describe the results of the numerical simulations.
These are as follows. For the left-handed screened equations, initialized by a disk and
an ellipse, we show merger and then un-merger as the ellipse rotates through the disk, in
complete analogy with the pure Eulerian dynamics of the unscreened equations. In the
right-handed unscreened case, initialized by two patch disks with the same sign, we observe
numerically translational merger. The patches travel along a common path and merge. When
the right-handed unscreened equations are initialized by two patch disks of opposite signs,
we observe orbiting patches deforming but not merging, indicating the possibility of (quasi)
periodic solutions. We also show that the symmetry that was proved rigorously in [9] to lead
to merger, does so numerically as well. Namely, we show that for right-handed unscreened
and screened equations, initialized by four disks, with the same sign across the horizontal
axis, and opposite sign across the vertical axis, there is merger across the horizontal axis.


                                 2. The active scalar system

   The sum and difference of the equations in the system (1) yield

             (∂t(F + ω) + {ψ + ϕ, F + ω} = 0,
                                                                                           (4)
                        ∂t(F −ω) + {ψ −ϕ, F −ω} = 0.

We define the scalars
                           σ± = F ± ω                                     (5)

and organize them into the vector

                           σ = (σ+, σ−).                                    (6)

The inertial MHD system (1) is thus equivalent to the transport equations

                         ∂tσ± + V±(σ) · ∇σ± = 0,                              (7)

where in the right-handed system

                        V±(σ) = U(ω) ± B(F),                               (8)

and in the left-handed system

                        V±(σ) = U(ω) ∓B(F).                               (9)

The operators are
                          U(ω) = ∇⊥(∆−1) ω,                             (10)

and
                       B(F) = ∇⊥(1 −∆)−1F.                           (11)
By construction, U, B and V± have divergence free ranges. The operator U is the Biot–Savart
law from the incompressible Euler equations, and the operator B is a screened version of
−U.

   The proof follows the pattern of the proof in [8], where the inviscid incompressible Euler
limit was proved in the Yudovich class. Instead of scalar equations, we are now considering
a system with two scalars. In addition, nontrivial adjustments need to be made because we
are operating in the whole space, not on the torus. The velocity associated to a Yudovich
σ, even if the latter is compactly supported, typically decays only like 1r, and thus fails to
be in L2. Also, a key element of [8] was the global exponential integrability of gradients
of velocity, which must be replaced here by local integrability. The strategy of proof is the
same though. We prove a short time convergence on any time interval of order cΩ−1∞if the
initial data converge in L2 and have the properties stemming from Proposition 1. We obtain
the long time limit by taking many steps (of the order TΩ∞). If we start from the same
initial data, the inviscid solution will be compactly supported in BT and the viscous solution
will obey (17) and in particular it will be exponentially small outside the fixed larger set
D = DT , for all t ≤T. We use these attributes of the solutions throughout the proof. We
prove the result for the right-handed system. The proof for the left handed system is the
same.

                                 5. The numerical algorithm

    In this section, we validate numerically the theoretical finding of merger in the odd-even
symmetry case, and explore potential mergers for other initial conditions. First, analytical
representations are derived for four different initial conditions, as the basis for our numerical
approximation. The approximation techniques we use are based on [11].

===== PDF PAGE 23 =====

   The initial boundaries of the vortex patches are closed curves in R2, which we parame-
terize clockwise for α ∈[0, 2π]. We linearly interpolate the curves with N nodes, which
correspond to our discretization of the boundaries of our patches. The nodes are initially
evenly distributed in α, and the contour is remeshed every 5 time steps in order to maintain
the uniform density of nodes (in α). We then evolve the patches discretely using second-order
finite differences to compute the differential contour element, second-order quadrature in α,
and a fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme for the time evolution.
    In order to satisfy the CFL condition, we choose time-step:

                                      1

    All numerical tests exhibit short-time convergence. We see no difference in behavior
of patches evolved with ∆t = N1 and ∆t = 10N1   . As in [11], we see that if there are too
few nodes along the contour or if these nodes are spaced too far apart, the evolution of the
discretized contour exhibits instability. After some time dependent on the initialization of the
patches, the simulations become inaccurate as small scales form and the contour filaments.
The numerical results we shall report are well before this time by an order of magnitude. In
the results we report, the number of nodes is fixed to N = 512.
