Abstract
Magnetic reconnection in W7-X is studied by means of global numerical simulations in a
series of models of increasing complexity. The magnetic geometry ranges from that of a
cylinder to the full three-dimensional field of W7-X, and the equations solved range from ideal
magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) to gyrokinetics. We simulate plasmas from the first operation
phase with electron cyclotron current drive (ECCD). These are characterized by an
equilibrium magnetic field featuring an ECCD-distorted ‘humped’ profile of the rotational
transform ι, with ι = 1 in two radial locations. Such plasmas generally show sawtooth activity,
hence motivating the present study. We pay particular attention to the role of equilibrium
current density gradients in the destabilization of reconnecting modes. When the equilibrium
temperature and density gradients are artificially suppressed (to eliminate the pressure gradient
drive), the perturbed electrostatic potential is radially localized between the locations at which
ι = 1. This is shown with a purely collisionless gyrokinetic model, in cylindrical geometry. In
the real toroidal geometry of W7-X, for a non-ideal MHD model including a uniform
resistivity, electron inertia and (numerical) viscosity, the same qualitative behaviour is
observed. In particular, even if a resonant (m, n) = (1, −1) perturbation is initialized, the most
unstable mode is the (m, n) = (−4, 4), where m and n are the poloidal and toroidal mode
numbers, respectively. Other modes are destabilized due to geometric coupling. The growth
rate of this instability scales as η1/3, where η is the plasma resistivity, thus suggesting that
ECCD drives ideal MHD stable W7-X plasmas towards non-ideal marginality. An ideal
magnetohydrodynamic analysis confirms the result. A fluid-kinetic hybrid version of the
EUTERPE code shows that gyrokinetic ions have a stabilizing effect on these modes. For
W7-X relevant collisionalities, the growth rate scales linearly with the electron skin depth, de.
Implications of our results for sawtoothing W7-X operation are discussed.

1. Introduction
Wendelstein 7-X [1–3] is a superconducting optimized stel-
larator [4], and, as such, not only enjoys low levels of neoclas-
sical transport [5], but is also expected to feature favourable
ideal magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) stability properties.
Transport studies have been undertaken to quantify the quality
of energy confinement [6–9], but enough heating power is not
yet available to access the pressure (β) limit in most magnetic
configurations, which is of the order of ⟨β⟩∼5% [10].
W7-X is a device conceptually rooted in certain key com-
putational and theoretical ideas [11], in particular quasi-
isodynamicity and optimization of MHD equilibrium and
stability, but the experimental programme sometimes poses
additional operational complications. For instance, periodic
and repetitive crashes of the central electron temperature, sim-
ilar to sawtooth crashes in tokamaks, emerged during divertor
strikeline control experiments with electron cyclotron current
drive (ECCD) [12]. Measurements from soft x-ray tomogra-
phy and electron cyclotron emission showed that the crashes
are preceded by weak oscillating precursors and a displace-
ment of the plasma core consistent with an (m, n) = (1, −1)
mode, with m and n being the dominant poloidal and toroidal
Fourier harmonics. This displacement occurs within 100 μs,
followed by expulsion and redistribution of the core into the
external part of the plasma. Furthermore, two types of crashes,
with different frequencies and amplitudes were detected.
The emergence of sawteeth [13–16] in W7-X has therefore
generated new interest in magnetic reconnection in stellara-
tors. Strumberger and G¨unter have presented comprehensive
linear stability studies of resistive modes for W7-X-type stel-
larator equilibria with ECCD [17]. Nonlinear simulations of
W7-X-like ι-profiles in a cylinder have also been performed
with a two-fluid model [18]. The same type of ι-profiles have
been investigated to study the possibility of kinetic destabi-
lization of infernal modes [19] (thus renewing interest also for
kinetic studies of q-profiles with reversed shear in tokamaks
[20]). Nikulsin et al [21] have proposed an extension of the
JOREK code [22] to stellarator geometry,in order address non-
linear magnetic reconnection physics in W7-X, and the non-
ideal reduced MHD equations for stellarator geometry have
also been studied analytically [23].
W7-X was optimized for ideal MHD stability, but the ques-
tion of non-ideal stability can also be important, in the light
of the experimental observations mentioned above. Resistive
and two-fluid effects [24–26] affect the linear stability thresh-
old and are sometimes able to predict nonlinear saturated
magnetic islands correctly [27, 28], but are not sufficient to
capture certain kinetic phenomena like collisionless heating
[28, 29] or the collisionless damping of the shear Alfv´en con-
tinuum [30]. W7-X plasmas are hot enough [19] that the resis-
tive reconnection scale, δη ≈5 × 10−3 cm, is much smaller
than the ion Larmor radius ρi ≈0.2 cm and the electron
skin depth de ≈0.2 cm. A realistic description of magnetic
reconnection in W7-X should therefore be kinetic.
A key ingredient in magnetic reconnection studies is played
by the profile of the rotationaltransform ι, which indicates how
many poloidal turns a field line makes during each toroidal
turn around the flux surface. Reconnection occurs when ι is
rational, and the current density gradient then provides one of
the two drives for reconnecting instabilities (the other being
the pressure gradient). Knowledge of the actual experimental
ι-profile is difficult to obtain, particularly in a stellarator, since
it is a global quantity that depends on the field-line geometry
throughout the entire plasma volume. Fortunately, however,
numerical transport modelling of the toroidal current evolu-
tion resulting from ECCD and the bootstrap current can pro-
vide some guidance. Such calculations suggest that ECCD can
result in a profile where two resonant flux surfaces with ι = 1
appear within the plasma, see e.g. the equivalent of our figure
3 in reference [12].
In the development of sawtooth dynamics in W7-X, which
is sudden and characterized by two different types of crashes
[12], the ι-evolution seems to regulate the access to differ-
ent, but co-existing, unstable scenarios. In this article, we
focus on the effect of the equilibrium current density gradients
for a resonant ι-profile. The study of pressure-gradient-driven
modes (not necessarily restricted to low mode numbers), is left
for a future companion article, where kinetic effects will be
investigated further.
In this work, by using the global codes GYGLES [31, 32]
and EUTERPE [33], we simulate typical plasmas from the first
operation phase in W7-X with ECCD. Our simulations fea-
ture several levels of complexity, both concerning physics and
geometry. In addition to ideal MHD computations, we have
used a purely collisionless gyrokinetic model in cylindrical
geometry,and a non-ideal fluid model including uniform resis-
tivity, electron inertia, (numerical) viscosity and gyrokinetic
ions, in real W7-X geometry.
With two kinetic species, and in cylindrical geometry, it is
found that the instability occurs after collisionless damping of
the shear Alfv´en continuum takes place. The radial positions
at which ι = 1 play an important role in the mode localiza-
tion, and for the strength of the instability. Qualitatively the
same localization are observed in cylindrical and stellarator
geometry. With the non-ideal fluid model, in W7-X, long-
wavelength reconnecting instabilities for an ECCD-distorted
ι-profile show growth rates that follow the scaling [34] η1/3,
where η is the plasma resistivity, thus suggesting that ECCD
drives ideally MHD stable W7-X plasmas towards non-ideal
marginality. This hypothesis is confirmed by an ideal MHD
study performed with the stability code CAS3D [35], which
indeed predicts ideal stability in W7-X. Moreover, the ideal

The article is structured as follows. Ideal MHD stabil-
ity properties of the plasma equilibria, subsequently stud-
ied within the gyrokinetic picture, are discussed in section
2. Kinetic equations and numerical methods are presented in
section 3. Numerical results are given in section 4. Here, we
first discuss the effect of the ‘humped’ rotational transform in
a cylindrical plasma with two kinetic species. We then present
our magnetic reconnection study for the true W7-X geometry.
Conclusions are summarized in the final section.
2. Preliminaries: ideal MHD stability for W7-X-like
ECCD ι-profiles
Before presenting our gyrokinetic and nonideal analysis, we
must ask ourselves what does ideal MHD say about W7-X-
like ι-profiles modified by ECCD, and whether that is enough
to understand the real W7-X stability properties under saw-
toothing conditions. Let us therefore consider the stability of
two types of plasma equilibria by linear ideal mode analysis
based on the ideal MHD energy principle [36, 37]. Magnetic
equilibria are evaluated with the VMEC equilibrium code [38,
39], whereas the stability analysis is performed with the eigen-
value code CAS3D [35]. Both codes are used in their free-
boundary versions. A detailed description of the combined
equilibrium/stability analysis procedure is given in reference
[40].
Firstly, the stability properties of an internal kink are stud-
ied in a screw-pinch equilibrium with all basic parameters cho-
sen similar to W7-X values. While it is known that toroidal
geometry has a critical impact on the stability of the linear
ideal MHD kink mode [41], the cylindrical limit is still impor-
tant if one aims at a non-ideal understanding of the sawtooth
phenomenon in W7-X, let alone to disentangle the small scales
present in the system [23]. The full equilibrium description is
given in appendix A, where table 2 summarizes the parameters
of the rotational transform profile in (A1) including the ι-hump
modelling ECCD. Notice that in all the MHD simulations the
pressure gradient is not artificially suppressed. For the screw
pinch studied here, the rotational transform is given by
ι(r) = 0.88 + 0.115(r/a)2 + fexp exp[−(r/a −sc)2/σ], (1)
with r/a the normalized minor radius and sc the center
of the hump. The resonance condition in straight-field-line
coordinates, k∥= m ι + n = 0, shows that perturbations with
poloidal and toroidal Fourier harmonics,m = −n, are resonant
at the two locations where ι = 1. Due to the cylindrical geom-
etry and the linear nature of the equations, all perturbation har-
monics may be studied separately in the stability calculation.
Here, the focus is on m = 1 modes. Variations of the rota-
tional transform are studied by changing (i) the radial location
sc and (ii) the height of the of the ι-hump controlled by fexp.
The parameter σ is adjusted to have the locations of ι = 1 at a
distance of 0.2 in the normalized minor radius. The various set-
tings of these ι-profile parameters are summarized in table 2.
The correspondingprofiles are shown in the left frame of figure
1, with the ι-hump centers at the radial locations sc ≈0.3, 0.4,
and 0.5, fixing fexp to 0.2. By way of example, the normal dis-
placementfor the latter case, sc ≈0.5, is shown, too, with clear
signatures of an internal kink mode being confined between
the resonances and having steep gradients at these locations.
The convergence of the instability growth rate with the radial
grid size is shown in the right frame of figure 1. The closer
the ι-hump comes to the magnetic axis, the smaller the growth
rate becomes. In the cases considered here it varies between
1.4 (ms)−1 for sc ≈0.5 and 0.86 (ms)−1 for sc = 0.3. Obvi-
ously, reducing the height of the ι-hump also has a stabilising
effect. With sc ≈0.5 the growth rate decreases to 0.95 (ms)−1
when changing fexp from 0.2 to 0.12, a reference value of the
gyrokinetic calculation of section 4.1. Transforming the ideal-
MHD growth rate to the units used in the kinetic case, yields
950 s−1. As we shall see, this compares well to the gyrokinetic
result, the ideal MHD result being more unstable.
Secondly, with the parameter set-up described in appendix
A, a finite-plasma-β W7-X-type equilibrium is considered for
its ideal MHD properties. In section 4.2, this equilibrium will
again be studied using a hybrid fluid-kinetic model. Three
characteristic plasma cross-sections of the equilibrium are
shown in figure 7, at the beginning, at a quarter, and half of
a field period of the five-periodic plasma domain.
Unlike the screw-pinch, stellarator fields possess rotational
transform already without a net toroidal current (black dotted
line in figure 2). This rotational transform profile is modified
with an ι-hump, which is here centered at the radial location
sc ≈0.4 and with ιmax = 1.02, to again model ECCD (solid
black line in figure 2). The result of a free-boundary calcula-
tion in which the plasma boundaryis allowed to move is shown
in figure 2. Each of the three scalar displacement components
is approximated employing 166 Fourier harmonics belonging
to the so-called N = 1 mode family [10], with the poloidal
Fourier harmonics 0 ⩽m ⩽19, the toroidal Fourier harmon-

perturbed magnetic field is used as norm for the eigenmodes,
facilitating the determination of stability limits without the
interference of the stable ideal MHD continua [42]. The equi-
librium is found to be stable with the chosen parameter setting
concerningflux- and Fourier resolution and mode family of the
perturbations. Figure 2 shows the ten largest Fourier harmon-

m = −n = 6, 4, 9, and 11amplitudes are strongest. Stellarator-
type helical equilibrium terms couple the m = −n = 6 and
m = −n = 11 harmonics and, likewise, the m = −n = 4 and
m = −n = 9 harmonics. Comparing the m = −n = 6 and
m = −n = 11 amplitudes, the latter is smaller only because
of the finite size of the perturbation Fourier table. In figure
2, the dominant harmonics show clear signs of an internal
kink with only small side-bands. The most important of the
side-bands with amplitudes about a factor of ten smaller than

5. Discussion and perspectives
In this article we have studied current-gradient-driven long-
wavelength reconnectingmodes for W7-X relevant conditions,
applying a hierarchy of numerical codes to describe the broad
range of relevant physical properties characterising sawtooth-
ing plasmas. On the most complete level, gyrokinetics was
applied to all species to investigate the role of the rotational
transform, ι, in driving magnetic reconnection. This was done
with the gyrokinetic (radially) global code GYGLES, in cylin-
drical geometry. In this case, we can observe the destabiliza-
tion of a ‘double-kink’ mode, emerging after the decay of the
shear Alfv´en continuum excited through the initial condition.
The instability is more violent, the more plasma is involved,
that is when the resonant location is increasingly far from the
magnetic axis. This property derives from the MHD character
of the instability, as the ideal MHD computations of section 2
show. The same qualitative mode structure is also found in real
W7-X geometry. For this case we used the gyrokinetic (radi-
ally) global code EUTERPE, in its reduced and hybrid-fluid
versions. The scaling of the long-wavelength reconnecting
instability with collisionality and electron mass is the derived.
It follows the typical scaling laws of the cylindrical limit, as
recently derived also for stellarators [23]. More importantly,
the scaling suggests that W7-X, when operating with ECCD,
is driven close to ideal marginality, and therefore becomes
susceptible to non-ideal instabilities. An ideal MHD analysis
performed with the CAS3D code confirms this results. Specifi-
cally, the dominant Fourier harmonics of the ideal mode found
by CAS3D to be closest to marginality are those first observed
with the code EUTERPE to be non-ideally destabilised. This is
useful to know, especially for future studies of pressure-driven
instabilities (whether reconnecting or not). Preliminary com-
putations suggest that the pressure-gradient drive can be quite
significant in plasmas with rotational transform profiles of the
type considered above. When kinetic effects are accounted
for, the instability threshold can be below the ideal one. A
summary of the most important results is given in table 1.
The existence of the instabilities investigated in this paper
has several practical implications. Firstly, they limit the param-
eter space available for safe device operation, if ECCD is
present, since the very largest crashes encompass the entire
plasma. For a crash of this type, the diamagnetic plasma
current suddenly disappears and a corresponding current in
any surrounding conductors is induced. Ensuing forces could
cause damage, and they should be assessed. Secondly, smaller-
amplitude sawtooth oscillations cause a degradation of con-
finement, just like in tokamaks, by expelling the hot plasma
from the plasma core. Thirdly, for future stellarators the exis-
tence of reconnectinginstabilities raises the concern that a low-
shear device is, in a sense, a delicate object, which can easily
be affected by a small amount of an externally driven toroidal
current, in particular if the latter is radially localized. However,
we must point out that, in the absence of a current drive, the
presence of an ι = 1 surface within the plasma does not pose
a limit, nor a risk, for plasma operations. This has been proven
in a series of ι−scan experiments in W7-X, in which the pres-
ence of rational surfaces affects, or even improves, but does
not limit confinement [63]. Finally, we must point out that the
understanding of the physics of crashes offers also the oppor-
tunity to control them, with all their properties. The clearest
examples in which sawtooth crashes can be useful would be
the removal of impurities from the core, or their exploitation
for improved edge plasma fueling.
