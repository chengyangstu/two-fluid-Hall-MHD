ABSTRACT
We deﬁne an endogenous magnetic reconnection process as the one having a driving factor that lies within the region where a drastic change
in magnetic ﬁeld topology occurs. A process of this kind is shown to take place when an electron temperature gradient is present in a plasma
column and when, referring to quasi-collisionless regimes, the evolving electron temperature ﬂuctuations are anisotropic. A two-ﬂuid theory,
for weakly collisional plasma regimes and conﬁnement conﬁgurations which have sheared magnetic ﬁelds, is formulated, and two classes of
reconnecting modes are identiﬁed. The localized class of modes is characterized by a transverse reconnecting magnetic ﬁeld ~Bx of odd parity,
as a function of the radial variable (across the reconnection layer). The width of this remains signiﬁcant even when large macroscopic distan-
ces, such as those of interest to space and astrophysics, are considered. The relevant reconnection can be referred to as antisymmetric. In
fact, the (additional and realistic) presence of a ﬁnite current density gradient leads to ﬁnding modes with mixed ~Bx parity which are no lon-
ger localized within the reconnection layer. Then, the resulting magnetic islands are not symmetric. In view of the fact that there are plasmas
in the Universe with considerable electron thermal energy contents, the features of the novel reconnecting modes can be relied upon in order
to produce generation of magnetic ﬁelds or conversion of magnetic energy into particle energy (through a sequence of mode-particle
resonances).

I. INTRODUCTION
The main characteristic of an endogenous magnetic reconnection
process is that its driving factor lies within the layer where a drastic
change in magnetic ﬁeld topology occurs. This kind of process is
shown to take place when an electron temperature gradient is present
in a magnetically conﬁned plasma and when, referring to quasi-
collisionless regimes, the evolving electron temperature ﬂuctuations
are anisotropic.1 In contrast to this, well known reconnection pro-
cesses such as those represented by the Sweet-Parker model or the
resistive internal kink mode2 are driven by factors (e.g., ﬂows or
plasma pressure gradients) that are outside the reconnection region. A
two-ﬂuid theory of the endogenous reconnection that is applicable to
weakly collisional plasma regimes is developed for the conﬁnement
conﬁguration with signiﬁcant rates of magnetic ﬁeld shear. Then,3 two
classes of reconnecting modes are identiﬁed. The localized class of
modes involves a transverse reconnected ﬁeld ~Bx of odd parity (as a
function of the radial variable) that is in contrast to the commonly
considered reconnecting modes and has characteristic phase velocities
and growth rates associated with a ﬁnite effective resistivity. The width
of the relevant reconnection layer remains signiﬁcant even when large
macroscopic distances are considered. The other class of modes is
characterized by ~Bx of even parity within the reconnection region and
extends well beyond it. In fact, the presence of a ﬁnite current density
gradient leads to ﬁnding modes that are composites of odd and even
parity ~Bx. In view of the fact that there are plasmas in the Universe
with considerable electron thermal energy contents, these features of
the considered modes can be relied upon in order to produce genera-
tion of magnetic ﬁelds or conversion of magnetic energy into particle
energy when the coupling of the localized odd modes to the extended
even modes can be signiﬁcant.
An illustrative example of endogenous reconnection is that of
“electrostatic” resistive modes whose growth rate is due to the product
of the local density gradient and gravitational acceleration. Although
in this case, the reconnected ﬁeld is relatively small, it is necessary to

We suggest that a sequence of mode-particle resonances4 starting
from the excitation of the oscillatory modes considered here may be
proposed5 for the generation of experimentally observed high energy
electron populations following magnetic reconnection events. An
attractive feature of processes of this kind is that the considered modes
involve relatively large amounts of particle thermal energies and can
channel the energy gained, with their excitation, into that of very few
particles.
The modes that we have found have signiﬁcant phase velocities1,5
with characteristic magnitudes and directions. Therefore, with their
excitation, these modes acquire momentum in the direction of the
main magnetic ﬁeld component. Then, the main body of the plasma
column is expected to recoil in the opposite direction and the extracted
momentum to be transported away from it by the same modes. When
these considerations are transferred to toroidal conﬁnement conﬁgura-
tions, we may argue that “reconnecting” modes excited within the
main body of the plasma can induce a “spontaneous rotation” of it.6
Relevant experimental observations7 are consistent with this theoreti-
cal indication. A procedure to evaluate the momentum of the modes,
which are standing in the x-direction and propagating in the y and z
directions, in the considered simpliﬁed plane geometry, was outlined
already in Ref. 5.
This paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we introduce the
simpliﬁed equilibrium plasma and ﬁeld conﬁgurations that are
adopted in our analysis. In Sec. III, we describe the perturbed state that
leads to the excitation of modes involving endogenous magnetic
reconnection. In Secs. IV and V, we discuss the basic equations that
are relevant to the evolution of the perturbed state and are capable of
describing the reconnection producing modes of interest. These equa-
tions are the electron thermal energy balance equations, the total
momentum conservation equation, the electron longitudinal momen-
tum conservation equation, and the electron mass conservation equa-
tion that are relevant to the weakly collisional plasma regimes
considered here. In the present paper, we simplify these equations by
keeping only the most important factors that drive the endogenous
reconnection process and provide relevant analytical solutions of
them. The expressions for the perturbed transverse and longitudinal
(to the equilibrium magnetic ﬁeld) electron temperatures that are con-
sidered to be relevant to quasi-collisionless regimes are given in Sec.
IV. In Sec. V, the relevant total momentum conservation equation and
the adopted longitudinal electron momentum conservation equation,
where the perturbed electron density is determined from a simpliﬁed
electron mass conservation equation, are presented. In Sec. VI, the
basic equation, for modes that are nearly electrostatic, are endogenous
(radially localized within the reconnection layer), and describe the
onset of antisymmetric reconnection, is given. In Sec. VII, resistive
overstable modes are found for the case where a signiﬁcant effective
electrical resistivity could be present and be compatible with the
anisotropy of the electron temperature ﬂuctuations. In Sec. VIII, an
analysis of the corresponding modes that are found in the case where
the ion diamagnetic frequency and the electron “drift” frequency can
be neglected is given and the limits of its validity are pointed out. In
Sec. IX, two kinds of endogenous modes are found: one with a phase
velocity in the direction of the ion diamagnetic velocity and one in the
opposite (electron) direction. For the latter kind of mode, the width of
the reconnection layer is found to be independent of the inductivity
width and to have physically signiﬁcant values. In Sec. X, we present a
simple and illustrative example of endogenous reconnection that
involves nearly electrostatic gravitational resistive modes. Since we
may associate the tapping of the magnetic ﬁeld energy by modes for
which the physical factor / dJz=dx is important, we address its effects
in Sec. XI after observing that the symmetry of the total momentum
conservation equation is broken due to the presence of the term
/ ðdJz=dxÞ. Consequently, modes that do not have a deﬁnite parity
and are described by eigenfunctions that are composites of odd and
even parity have to be considered. Conclusions are given in Sec. XII.

XII. CONCLUSIONS
A major ﬁnding of the present paper is that magnetic ﬁeld
ﬂuctuations can be generated in the presence of a signiﬁcant elec-
tron temperature gradient, a reconnection process that converts
electron thermal energy into magnetic ﬁeld energy. We note that
the standard theoretical model for the origin of the cosmic mag-
netic ﬁelds is the generation of weak seed magnetic ﬁelds due to
the “Biermann Battery” effect, and then the ampliﬁcation of the
seed ﬁelds via turbulent dynamo mechanism to the level consistent
with the current observations.10 Since there are plasmas in the
Universe with considerable electron thermal energy contents, we
suggest that the reconnection process described in the present

paper may also play a signiﬁcant role in the origin of the observed
strong cosmic magnetic ﬁelds.
Referring to the Earth’s magnetosphere there is real evidence of
collisonless magnetic reconnection processes in the presence of elec-

here needs to be extended to ﬁeld geometries that are relevant to ﬁeld

temperature gradients is considered,13 from which reconnecting
modes can emerge. We observe that in Ref. 14 a magnetic ﬁeld geome-
try that should be close to those inferred from the observations in the
Earth’s magnetotail is adopted.
The best agreement between theory and experiments concerning
the onset of magnetic reconnection in laboratory plasmas is (probably)
represented by the theory of the internal kink mode.2 A proposed
explanation of the observed accelerated reconnection rate following
the onset15 is that, in this case, reconnection involves the formation of
a relatively large magnetic island and a local steepening of the electron
temperature gradient. Then, the effects of the endogenous reconnect-
ing mode associated with electron temperature gradient are com-
pounded with those (e.g., total plasma pressure gradient) that lead to
the onset of reconnection.
Endogenous modes associated with a ﬁnite electron temperature
gradient may be sustained by the electron temperature heating rate
due to the charged reaction products in a fusion burning plasma.16 In
this case, the longitudinal thermal conductivity on selected rational
magnetic surfaces,16 referring to a toroidal conﬁnement conﬁgura-
tions, is decreased, relative to its collisional value, by the effects of
reconnection.
