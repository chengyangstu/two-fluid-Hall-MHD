                                         Magnetic reconnection is a universal process that rapidly releases magnetic
          Check for updates                   energy in plasmas, driving explosive phenomena in both space and laboratory
                                           environments. Yet a complete understanding of how reconnection proceeds in1234567890():,;                 1234567890():,;                                              large-scale plasmas remains elusive. Here we show that expanding bifurcated
                                             current sheets (EBCS) are a recurring structure of quasi-steady reconnection.
                                    EBCS are double-peaked current layers that expand along the boundaries of
                                           the reconnection outﬂow, accompanied by localized electric ﬁelds and strong
                                            ion acceleration. We identify this structure consistently in laboratory experi-
                                          ments, spacecraft observations near Earth and the Sun, and large-scale kinetic
                                               simulations. Our results indicate that EBCS arise from two-ﬂuid plasma
                                        dynamics and occur during quasi-steady antiparallel reconnection, particu-
                                                       larly for plasmas with moderate plasma beta (0.1 < β ≲1, where β is the ratio of
                                        plasma thermal pressure to magnetic pressure). These ﬁndings link laboratory
                                      and space observations to the classical Petschek model and show how two-
                                          ﬂuid dynamics can produce open reconnection outﬂows across widely dif-
                                               ferent system sizes.
