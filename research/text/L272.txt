The origin of extreme and anomalous ion heating during magnetic reconnection has been a longstanding
mystery. It is shown in this paper that, at the core of the process, the stochastic heating mechanism is
responsible for this energization. The in-plane Hall electric ﬁelds that intrinsically develop during collisionless
reconnection not only accelerate ions, but also thermalize them by inducing chaotic motion. The mechanism
explains various observed features of ion heating, e.g., its relation to the guide ﬁeld strength and the ion
mass-to-charge ratio.

VI.
DISCUSSION
327
In Sec. I, ﬁve main features of ion heating during col-
328
lisionless reconnection were identiﬁed. Each of these fea-
329
tures will now be individually examined in relation to the
330
stochastic heating mechanism.
331
1. The association of anomalous ion heating with Hall
332
electric ﬁelds. It has been shown that Hall electric
333
ﬁelds intrinsically satisfy the stochastic ion heat-
334
ing criterion during collisionless magnetic reconnec-
335
tion for up to moderate guide ﬁelds (bg ∼0.5);
336
the electric ﬁelds thus render ion motions chaotic
337
and eﬀectively heat the species. In particular, Hall
338
electric ﬁelds as well as ion heating were measured
339
in situ in the Magnetic Reconnection Experiment
340
(MRX)10 during a driven collisionless reconnection
341
event. Inserting the measured values of E ∼700
342
V/m, B ∼0.01 T and a length scale of ∼1 cm
343
yields α = 14.6 > 1, so the fact that Hall electric
344
ﬁelds induce stochastic ion heating is well evidenced
345
by experiments.
346
2. Violation of the magnetic moment µ conservation
347
law.
Stochastic heating is a mechanism wherein
348
the guiding-center approximation breaks down as
349
in Sec. II, in which case the magnetic moment µ
350
is obviously not conserved. In fact, it was shown
351
via test-particle simulations32 that regions where µ
352
is not conserved coincide with regions where the
353
stochastic ion heating condition is satisﬁed, and
354
that initially close particles diverge in time—i.e.,
355
the system has a positive Lyapunov exponent.
356
3. Temperature anisotropy. Stochastic heating is as-
357
sociated with perpendicular motion and thus with
358
perpendicular temperature.
The perpendicular
359
energy gain, however, could eventually be redis-
360
tributed in the parallel direction through other
361
means such as collisions, waves, and/or instabili-
362
ties.
363
4. Reduction of the heating amount and extent with
364
an increasing guide ﬁeld. This correlation was pre-
365
dicted in Sec. IV and veriﬁed in Sec. V. In ad-
366
dition, the analysis in Sec. III also hints to this
367
correlation.
For instance, including a guide ﬁeld
368
Bz0 when inserting Eq. 37 into Eq. 1 yields the
369
condition,
370

371

372
5. Preferential heating of ions with higher mass-to-
373
charge ratios. The stochastic ion heating condition
374
(Eq. 1) is more easily satisﬁed for higher values of
375
mi/qi. The heating amount also increases, as evi-
376
denced by PIC simulations with a partial mixture
377
of heavy ions in Yoon and Bellan 32.
378
It should be mentioned that there may of course be other
379
reconnection situations where the dominant ion heating
380
mechanism is not stochastic heating.
These situations
381
may for example involve three-dimensional structures,
382
high plasma beta, and/or high guide ﬁeld strengths; as
383
mentioned in Sec. I, other mechanisms could then cause
384
the ion heating. Also, there may also be situations where
385
ion heating is due to stochastic heating but the source
386
for large α diﬀers (e.g., lower-hybrid drift waves43,44,51
387
in asymmetric reconnection52,53). However, for the text-
388
book two-dimensional collisionless reconnection geome-
389
try which has been the focus of numerous previous stud-
390
ies, stochastic ion heating is not only important but in-
391
trinsic to the process. Therefore, the attribution of ion
392
heating to the stochastic heating mechanism due to the
393
Hall electric ﬁelds must ﬁrst be considered when analyz-
394
ing any reconnection process.

397
