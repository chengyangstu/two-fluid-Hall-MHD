Abstract
15
In the diﬀusion region of magnetotail reconnection, particle distributions are highly struc-
16
tured, exhibiting triangular shapes and multiple striations that deviate dramatically from
17
the Maxwellian distribution. Fully kinetic simulations have been demonstrated to be ca-
18
pable of producing the essential structures of the observed distribution functions, yet are
19
computationally not feasible for 3D global simulations. The ﬂuid models used for large-
20
scale simulations, on the other hand, do not have the kinetic physics necessary for de-
21
scribing reconnection accurately. Our study aims to bridge fully kinetic and ﬂuid sim-
22
ulations by quantifying the information required to capture the non-Maxwellian features
23
in the distributions underlying the closures used in the ﬂuid code. We compare the re-
24
sults of fully kinetic simulations to observed electron velocity distributions in a magne-
25
totail reconnection diﬀusion region, and use the maximum entropy model to reconstruct
26
electron and ion distributions using various numbers of moments obtained from the sim-
27
ulation. Our results indicate that using only local moments, the maximum entropy model
28
can reproduce many of the features of the distributions: (1) the electron-outﬂow distri-
29
bution with a tilted triangular structure is reproduced with 21 or more moments in agree-
30
ment with Ng, Hakim, and Bhattacharjee [2018] and (2) counterstreaming distributions
31
can be captured with the 35-moment model when the separation in velocity space be-
32
tween the populations is large.

Plain Language Summary
34
Fluid modelling of plasmas in collisionless environments such as the magnetosphere
35
is challenging because the approximation that collisions are important does not hold. It
36
is important to understand the motion of individual particles as collisions do not bring
37
them back to equilibrium, However, this is computationally intensive and cannot be used
38
to model the Earth at the present time. We use a recent ﬂuid model which is based on
39
maximising the entropy of an underlying distribution of particles and attempt to recon-
40
struct particle distributions during reconnection in the Earth’s magnetotail. We show
41
that most features of the distribution function can be reproduced using an increasing num-
42
ber of ﬂuid moments.

1 Introduction
44
Non-Maxwellian particle velocity distributions prevail in weakly collisional plasma
45
environments such as the magnetosphere. In particular, for magnetic reconnection re-
46
gions in the magnetotail, strongly anisotropic electron velocity distributions with p∥>
47
p⊥are found in inﬂow regions [Øieroset, Lin, Phan, Larson, & Bale, 2002], while trian-
48
gular distributions with accelerated electrons are found within the electron diﬀusion re-
49
gion and close to the x-point during antiparallel reconnection [Torbert et al., 2018]. The
50
formation of these distributions can be explained by electron trapping in the inﬂow [Egedal,
51
Le, & Daughton, 2013], while in the electron diﬀusion region, the meandering electron
52
orbits in the magnetic ﬁeld reversal explains the striated and counterstreaming popu-
53
lations [Bessho, Chen, Shuster, & Wang, 2014; Bessho, Chen, Wang, & Hesse, 2018; Fu-
54
jimoto & Sydora, 2009; Ng, Egedal, Le, & Daughton, 2012; Ng, Egedal, Le, Daughton,
55
& Chen, 2011].
56
Although kinetic physics is necessary to capture these structures which are depen-
57
dent on the electron trajectories, fully kinetic simulations are computationally expen-
58
sive, and many global simulations of magnetospheres use ﬂuid models such as magne-
59
tohydrodynamics (MHD). There have been diﬀerent approaches to incorporate physics
60
beyond MHD in global simulations. This includes hybrid-PIC [Karimabadi, Vu, Krauss-
61
Varban, & Omelchenko, 2006; Lu et al., 2015] and Vlasov codes [Kempf et al., 2013; Palm-
62
roth et al., 2018], which simulate kinetic ions and ﬂuid electrons. Another approach in-
63
volves embedding PIC boxes within a larger-scale MHD or two-ﬂuid simulation, so that
64

only the regions in which kinetic eﬀects are thought to be important use a fully kinetic
65
model [Fujimoto, 2018; Ho, Datta, & Shumlak, 2018; Lautenbach & Grauer, 2018; T´oth
66
et al., 2017, 2016].
67
The approach discussed in this paper involves extended ﬂuid models which evolve
68
the higher moments of the Vlasov equation have been developed [Hesse, Winske, & Kuznetsova,
69
1995; Ng, Hakim, Bhattacharjee, Stanier, & Daughton, 2017; Wang, Hakim, Bhattachar-
70
jee, & Germaschewski, 2015; Yin, Winske, Gary, & Birn, 2001]. In addition to the usual
71
density and momentum moments, these models evolve the pressure tensor, which is im-
72
portant for balancing the reconnection electric ﬁeld at the x-point. Observational val-
73
idation tests of such models have produced encouraging results [Dong et al., 2019; Wang
74
et al., 2018]. However, closures for general plasma parameters are challenging to develop
75
and these models do not capture all the kinetic physics, which can aﬀect the reconnec-
76
tion rate and development of instabilities [Ng et al., 2017; Ng, Hakim, Juno, & Bhattachar-
77
jee, 2019].
78
In this paper we focus on what distribution function information can be obtained
79
given a ﬁxed number of known moments of velocity distribution functions. We use the
80
maximum entropy closure [Levermore, 1996], which has the useful property that the de-
81
rived ﬂuid equations are hyperbolic. Our earlier work on using this closure to reconstruct
82
electron distributions in reconnection regions has shown that the characteristic triangu-
83
lar shape can be recovered using 21 moments (the heat ﬂux tensor and scalar v4 moment)
84
[Ng et al., 2018]. In this study, we use parameters more relevant to the event reported
85
by Torbert et al. [2018], and the distributions in the simulation are chosen to correspond
86
to the observations. We also increase the number of moments kept to 35, thereby demon-
87
strating the ability of the model to recover counterstreaming populations.
88
The remainder of this paper is organised as follows: In Section 2 we review the deriva-
89
tion of the maximum closure and the ﬂuid equations obtained, compared to the Chapman-
90
Enskog and Grad approaches. In Section 3 we perform comparisons between the elec-
91
tron distribution functions obtained from MMS observations, a kinetic simulation and
92
reconstructions using successively higher numbers of moments. We then discuss the re-
93
sults in Section 4 and the implications for ﬂuid modelling.

2 Maximum entropy closure
95
Maximum entropy methods are used to determine probability distributions given
96
limited information [Jaynes, 1957], and have been used in many scientiﬁc ﬁelds includ-
97
ing astrophysics [Richstone & Tremaine, 1988], biology [Yeo & Burge, 2004] and natu-
98
ral language processing [Charniak, 2000]. In the context of ﬂuid dynamics, the maximum
99
entropy ﬂuid closure was ﬁrst derived in [Levermore, 1996] as an alternative to the tra-
100
ditional Chapman-Enskog and Grad approaches [Chapman & Cowling, 1970; Grad, 1949b].
101
It is a non-perturbative closure and is constructed to ensure the positivity of the distri-
102
bution function f and the hyperbolicity of the convective part of the ﬂuid equations ob-
103
tained. Here we brieﬂy review the derivation of the moment equations.
104

3 Reconstruction of distributions in reconnection regions
158
During the Magnetospheric Multiscale (MMS) encounter with an electron diﬀu-
159
sion region described in [Torbert et al., 2018], highly non-Maxwellian electron distribu-
160
tion functions were observed close to the x-point. These velocity distributions have been
161
studied in detail [Bessho et al., 2018] and it has been shown that the combination of the
162
electron meandering motion and reconnection electric ﬁeld is important for setting the
163
structure of the distribution functions. These distributions are used to identify regions
164
in the simulation for study and provide a link to reality.
165
We focus on understanding what aspects of the distribution function in this recon-
166
nection region successively higher moment models can capture compared to kinetic sim-
167
ulations. To that end, we use the three distributions corresponding to the inﬂow, below
168
the x-point and exhaust regions shown in Fig. 1, and perform fully kinetic particle-in-
169
cell simulations for comparison. The known moments are then used to reconstruct dis-
170
tributions using the maximum-entropy model for various numbers of moments. Aside
171
from the usual isotropic Maxwellian, we use the 10-, 14-, 21- and 35-moment models, which
172
have important properties in ﬂuid simulations. The 10-moment model is being used in
173
global simulations of magnetospheres [Dong et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2018] and includes
174
the pressure tensor, which is important for balancing the reconnection electric ﬁeld in
175
collisionless reconnection. The 14- and 21-moment models include the heat-ﬂux vector
176
and tensor respectively, and it has been shown that capturing the heat ﬂux correctly is
177
important for reproducing the reconnection rate observed in kinetic simulations [Allmann-
178
Rahn, Trost, & Grauer, 2018; Ng et al., 2017]. The 35-moment model extends our pre-
179
vious study [Ng et al., 2018] by including the tensor vvvv moment instead of a scalar
180
v4.

287
We have used successively higher numbers of moments to reconstruct electron ve-
288
locity distributions in reconnection regions. The maximum entropy method, which we
289
use, has the properties of guaranteeing the positivity of the resulting distribution and
290
the hyperbolicity of the ﬂuid equations obtained, but is more diﬃcult to evaluate as no
291
closed form solutions exist for > 10 moments. This closure reconstructs the distribu-
292
tion from only the locally known moments without information about the electromag-
293
netic ﬁelds or physical processes, allowing it to be used for diﬀerent plasma parameters.
