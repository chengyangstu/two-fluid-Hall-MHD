Simulations of current sheet thinning and reconnection
Lin Yin and Dan Winske
Applied Physics Division, Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, New Mexico, USA
Received 29 May 2002; revised 26 August 2002; accepted 12 September 2002; published 28 December 2002.
[1]
Thin current sheet structures with a thickness comparable to the ion gyro-radius
have been inferred from spacecraft observations during substorms. This study presents
two-dimensional hybrid and Hall-MHD simulations of current sheet thinning and
reconnection that include electron pressure tensor effects. Results demonstrate that
reconnection occurs due to the electron pressure tensor effects as the sheet is compressed
by the application of an out-of-plane electric field on the lobe boundaries. Results from
hybrid and Hall-MHD calculations for the current sheet thinning process are compared.
During the thinning period, the out-of-plane ion velocity from the hybrid simulation
shows an enhancement at the edge of the sheet and a reduction at the sheet center. As
reconnection proceeds, it becomes peaked along the Earth-tail direction away from the X
point. In contrast, the out-of-plane ion velocity from the Hall-MHD simulation remains
peaked at the center of the simulation. The ion off-diagonal pressure tensor effects are
considered as the cause of the differing particle-ion and fluid-ion dynamics. The
dynamics of current sheet thinning and reconnection is also modeled using a new
integrated approach in which Hall-MHD calculations are embedded inside an MHD
simulation. It is shown that the embedded simulation can provide smooth transitions of
plasma and field quantities between the two regions, and the small-scale physics in the
compressed current sheet and in the near-X-point region are well represented.

Introduction
[2] Thin current sheet structures have been observed in
the near-Earth, mid-tail, and distant tail regions during
substorms [Sergeev et al., 1993; Pulkkinen et al., 1993,
1994, 1999; Sanny et al., 1994]. It was inferred from the
measurements that current sheets can thin to a thickness
comparable to the ion gyro-radius (based on the magnetic
field strength outside the sheet). These thin current sheet
observations indicate a two-scale structure comprising a
thin current sheet with enhanced current density embed-
ded within a much thicker plasma sheet. Thin current
sheets have also been observed recently at high latitudes,
where the Polar spacecraft is at times able to remain in
the sheet for extended periods of time [Scudder et al.,
2002].
[3] Examining the mechanisms involved in the current
sheet thinning process is a subject of interest because of
its importance to the understanding of the substorm
dynamics. Various simulation studies, including full par-
ticle (kinetic ions and electrons), hybrid (kinetic ions and
massless fluid electrons), Hall-magnetohydrodynamics
(Hall-MHD), and large scale MHD simulations, have
demonstrated that thin current sheets form as a result of
the duskward external driving electric field imposed at the
lobe boundaries [Pritchett and Coroniti, 1994, 1995;
Hesse et al., 1996; Birn et al., 1998; Rasta¨tter et al.,
1999]. During the compression of the sheet, the magnetic
field component normal to the sheet reduces substantially
as the magnetic flux is transfered to the tail. As a result,
the thin current sheet configuration can become unstable
and reconnection may be initiated [Pritchett and Coroniti,
1995].
[4] The aforementioned scale length of the thin current
sheet and the two-scale structure inferred from observations
have been clearly demonstrated in full particle simulations
of thin current sheet formation [Pritchett and Coroniti,
1994, 1995]. It is difficult to perform a detailed study of
the current carriers from spacecraft measurements of thin
current sheets because of the coarse temporal resolution of
the data [Pulkkinen et al., 1994]. However, the simulation
results of Pritchett and Coroniti [1994, 1995] have revealed
that the enhanced current is carried by the electrons, which
undergo E  B drifts in the electrostatic electric field normal
to the sheet established during the course of thinning. These
results were also confirmed later by hybrid and Hall-MHD
simulations [Hesse et al., 1996; Rasta¨tter et al., 1999]. The

The advantage of this model is that it omits the electron
skin depth scale (c/wpe) structure of the dissipation region.
It is known that the c/wpe scale structure does not control
the rate of reconnection [Shay et al., 1998, 1999];
however, it demands high spatial resolution and therefore
a much greater computing effort. Such reduced models
include hybrid (particle ions) and Hall-MHD (two-fluid)
calculations [Hesse and Winske, 1994; Winske and Hesse,
1994; Yin et al., 2001a] that neglect the electron inertia
but employ a reduced kinetic electron description. The
essential electron physics near the X point can be repre-
sented well by resolving only ion spatial and temporal
scales.
[7] In this study these reduced models are used to
simulate the formation of thin current sheets in two spatial
dimensions. Both 1-D and 2-D current sheet equilibria
with a thicker current sheet are used and out-of-plane
electric fields are applied at the lobe boundaries to com-
press the sheet. The boundary driving resembles the
conditions that the magnetotail current sheet experiences
during the growth phase of a substorm. We compare the
ion motions from the hybrid and Hall-MHD calculations
and identify what is missing with the two-fluid approach.
We demonstrate that current sheet thinning from external
driving can lead to a natural initiation of reconnection as
the electron pressure tensor effects become important.
Hall-MHD simulations with electron pressure tensor
effects have not been used previously to address the
current sheet thinning issues. Hybrid simulation studies
of sheet thinning using the electron pressure tensor model
have been conducted earlier. However, as noted by Hesse
et al. [1996] these studies concern the dynamics of a large
section of the magnetotail and the numerical dissipation
associated with the coarse spatial resolution prevented the
onset of physical reconnection to be observed in the
simulations.
[8] Traditionally, plasma processes with very different
spatial and temporal scales are studied separately. Large
scale phenomena are typically modeled using MHD simu-
lations, whereas small-scale processes near the X point
require at least Hall-MHD calculations to capture the
important physics. The dynamics of lobe regions can be
well approximated by MHD calculations: Far from the
diffusion region the effects of Hall current and electron
pressure are insignificant, the ions and electrons follow the
motion of the field lines, and E = v  B (where vi = ve = v)
provides a good approximation. However, as the current
sheet thins to a thickness on the order of the ion gyro-radius,
the electron and ion motions become decoupled and the thin
sheet region should be modeled by two-fluid calculations.
While MHD and Hall-MHD simulations are both well
established tools for studying these the large and small
scale phenomena, attempts have not been made previously
to develop a simulation tool for collisionless reconnection in
which different sets of equations are solved in different
regions (i.e., an ‘‘embedded simulation’’). In this study we
examine the possibility of the embedded simulation
approach and present some initial results of our embedded
Hall-MHD/MHD simulations for the current sheet thinning
and reconnection problem.

effectively.
2.2.
Method of Embedding
[13] As a first attempt to investigate the micro- macro-
scale coupling in the magnetotail dynamics, we developed a
new integrated approach in which Hall-MHD calculations
with full electron pressure tensor model are embedded
inside an MHD simulation near the X point. A schematic
illustration of the approach is included in Figure 1. The
Hall-MHD calculations run on the inner region to model
correctly the ion inertial scale physics near the X point,
including the decoupling of the ion and electron motions
and the reconnection electric field produced by the off-
diagonal electron pressure tensor terms. The MHD code
runs on the outer region and calculates large scale fields and
one-fluid quantities. The MHD solutions also provide
appropriate boundary conditions for the two-fluid calcula-

3.3.
Embedding of Hall-MHD Calculations Within a
MHD Simulation
[27] We have discussed in section 3.2 that, in comparison
with the hybrid calculations, the Hall-MHD simulation with
the scalar ion pressure cannot describe the kinetic effects in
the ion out-of-plane motion and the correct ion current
profile. Nevertheless, the Hall-MHD simulation with the
full electron pressure model is capable of including the
essential electron physics near the X point for the current
sheet thinning and reconnection modeling. The external
driving perturbations applied at the lobe boundaries are
communicated to the inner region by fast compressional
waves which are represented in both MHD and Hall-MHD
calculations. Thus, one can embed Hall-MHD calculations
inside an MHD simulation to simulate current sheet thin-
ning and reconnection. In this section we present some
initial results of the embedded simulation effort.
[28] The initial equilibrium is 2-D with a finite normal
component Bx with e = 0.1 and n = 0.5. The initial value of
Bx/Bo in the center of the sheet at the left boundary is about
5% of the lobe field strength and its profile along y at the
center of the sheet (x = 10) is shown in Figure 7. The spatial
domain is 60  20 (c/wpi)2, much larger than the calcu-
lations presented earlier in this paper, and consists of 512 
256 grid points. The initial current sheet half thickness at
the left boundary is a = 4c/wpi, which corresponds to about
12.6 ion gyro-radii in the lobe field. The profile of the
imposed Ez on the x boundaries varies as cos [0.66p(y 
15)/60] (i.e., the maximum driving is at y = 15c/wpi). A
comparison of the in-plane magnetic field and out-of-plane
current density Jz from the Hall-MHD and embedded Hall-
MHD/MHD simulations is displayed in Figure 8 (the y-
boundary conditions bx = 0 and dby/dy = 0, where bx and by
are the perturbed magnetic fields, are used).
[29] The initial 2-D equilibrium is shown in the top panel.
The configurations from the two calculations during recon-
nection (at t ci = 190) after the compression of the sheet
under the boundary driving are shown in the middle and
bottom panels. For the embedded simulation shown in the
bottom panel, the inner Hall-MHD region extends from x =
5.5 to x = 14.5 and y = 0 to y = 50. The results indicate that
the transition from the Hall-MHD to the MHD region is
smooth. Both calculations show that as the sheet thins the
current density increases and the normal magnetic field Bx
decreases. At the present stage of the embedded code, the
grid size in the MHD region is the same as that in the Hall-
MHD region. It is straightforward, however, to modify the
embedded code so that the MHD region uses larger grids to
eliminate unnecessary calculations on the fine mash.
[30] Figure 9 shows the reconnected flux as a function of
time from the Hall-MHD and embedded Hall-MHD/MHD
simulations. The flux is computed from a time integration of
the out-of-plane electric field, REzdt, where Ez is averaged
over nine grids near the X point. It is seen that the two
curves are indistinguishable, which demonstrates the excel-
lent agreement between the two simulation runs.
